Privacy Policy — Livery Lab

Effective date: 14 November 2026.

One policy for every version of the app — iOS, Android and Windows. Where they behave differently, it is said so.

In short

Without an action of yours, Livery Lab receives neither your work nor your name and email: no livery contents, no images you placed in them. We receive them only if you sign in with Google (name and email), upload a livery to the cloud or make a link for a friend — see "Account", "Livery cloud and Pro" and "Link for a friend". There is no advertising.

What the app does send is usage statistics and crash reports: which actions were taken, from which country, region and city (worked out from the IP address), and whether the app crashed. No livery names, no file names and no images go there. What is sent is described in "Usage statistics and crash reports" below.

The one exception is a link for a friend, and only when you make one yourself: then a turntable of your car as images and the livery's captions go to our server, so that your friend can see them in a browser. Details are in "Link for a friend".

The second exception is the livery cloud (part of Pro), again only when you tap for it: a livery you put into the cloud yourself is kept on our server under your account so that you can open it on another device. Details are in "Livery cloud and Pro".

What is stored, and where

Everything you do in the app stays on your device:

  • the car models you added
  • liveries: layers, covers, team and driver names, race number
  • the images you placed as decals
  • the archives built for the game

The developer has no access to these files: the files themselves never leave the device unless you put a livery into the cloud yourself. Car models are downloaded from our server. What goes back from your device is the usage statistics, crash reports and service requests described below and, when you tap for it, the turntable for a link for a friend. Not one livery file, layer or model is among them.

On iOS the files live in the app's documents folder: you can see them in the Files app, and they go into your device backup if you have one enabled.

On Android the files live in the app's internal storage. Other apps cannot see them, and they do not go into a backup: app data backup is turned off (allowBackup="false").

Deleting the app deletes all of these files with it.

Network access

The app goes online in six places, and only these:

  1. The model catalog — reads the list of cars and downloads the one you picked. No liveries and no images are sent there. As with any website, the host sees your IP address while the file is downloading — the file could not reach you otherwise.
  2. Usage statistics — sends the events listed below.
  3. Remote settings — the app asks whether it is time to update. The request carries an anonymous installation number, the app and system versions and the device language — the server cannot answer without them. No name and none of your files are among them.
  4. A link for a friend — only when you tap "Share a link" or "Delete link". What exactly is sent is described in the next section.
  5. Account — when you sign in, sign out or delete it. While you are signed in, Firebase also renews the sign-in key from time to time. See "Account".
  6. Pro and the livery cloud — on every launch, and when you sign in or out, the app asks our server whether Pro is open. Without an account, only this question is sent. If you are signed in, the sign-in key goes too, and the server answers whether your account has Pro. The same happens when you open the Pro or cloud window. Once payments launch, the app will also check the subscription with Google Play on launch if you are signed in, and pass it to our server. The cloud list is read only when you are signed in: when you open the cloud window, before each livery upload, and in the "Delete account?" question — to say how many liveries will be erased. Liveries themselves go to the cloud and come back from it only when you tap for it. See "Livery cloud and Pro".

Items 5 and 6 are in the Android app. The iPhone app has no sign-in and no cloud yet, and does not go online for them.

The app makes no other network requests — not when parsing a model, not while painting, not when building the archive. The Windows version goes online differently — see “Windows version”.

On Android the app requests four permissions: internet access and network state — for everything listed above; Google Play billing (BILLING) — for the Pro subscription; and a Google services permission (READ_GSERVICES) that comes with Firebase's protection of sign-in against bots (reCAPTCHA). There is also the app's own internal permission, added by an Android library: it stops other apps from sending the app service signals and opens access to nothing. The app asks for no camera, no microphone, no location (the statistics server works out the country and city from the IP address — see "Usage statistics and crash reports"), no contacts and no photo library — you pick the model and the images through the system file picker, which needs none.

Link for a friend

"Share a link" makes a page where a friend, without the app, sees your car in its livery and can turn it around. Nothing is sent until you tap that button yourself.

What is sent. A turntable — 48 images of your car from all sides plus a cover, made by the app — and captions for the page: the livery and car names, the game, the number, and the team and driver names if you entered them. Livery files, layers, the car model and the original decal images are not sent. If your livery carries your own photo as a decal, it shows on the turntable just as it does on the body.

Where it is stored. On our server liverylab.uz, which runs on Cloudflare (a processor acting on our behalf): the turntable is kept in Cloudflare R2 storage.

Who can see it. Anyone who has the link. The page is closed to search engines, but the address can be forwarded — do not share what you do not want to show.

How to delete it. With "Delete link" in the app: the turntable and the captions are erased at once, and the old address only shows "This livery was removed". The deletion key is kept in the app on your device; if the app is gone, email us at the address below with the link and we will delete it by hand.

Reports. The page has a "Report" button. A report keeps only its time and the reason, if one was given — not the address or the device of the person reporting. Links that break the rules or someone's rights are taken down.

A link is not tied to your account, even if you are signed in: no name or email goes to the page or the links server. Like any website, the server sees the IP address of whoever uploads a turntable or opens the page — only for the duration of the request and to allow no more than five links a minute from one address; we do not store it. Everything travels over HTTPS.

Account

Signing in is optional: you can paint, build the archive and put a livery into the game without an account. You may sign in if you wish — with Google, using the "Account" button in the garage, in the Android app and the Windows program. The iPhone app has no sign-in yet. The account carries the livery cloud and the Pro subscription — continuing liveries on other devices.

What we receive. The name and email address that Google provides, the sign-in method and the account number assigned by Firebase. No password, no liveries and no files go into the account.

Where it is kept. In Google Firebase Authentication (a processor acting on our behalf). The device keeps a sign-in key so you do not have to sign in every time; on a computer it is kept together with the name and email, in the app's folder.

On a computer sign-in happens in the browser: the app opens a sign-in page at auth.liverylab.uz, and the page hands the sign-in key back to the app on the same computer (address 127.0.0.1). The browser does not remember the sign-in itself.

The account is not tied to links for a friend or to crash reports. It is tied to the usage statistics only by a pseudonymous number, described in "Usage statistics and crash reports"; your name, your email and the Firebase account number itself do not go there. No selling, no advertising.

Livery cloud and Pro

The cloud is optional and only for signed-in users: a livery goes there only when you tap "To cloud" or "Upload a livery" yourself, and only the one you picked.

What is sent. The livery file as it is: layers and masks, the cover, the livery name, team and driver names, the number. The images placed on it as decals — together with the original names of their files. For text decals — the text itself and its styling: colors, outline, curve, font. A description of the car — its name, its number in our catalog, the game; for ACC, the model number in the game; for the original Assetto Corsa, the car's folder in the game and the name of the body texture. Along with the livery go the time it was changed and the kind of device (iPhone, Android or computer). The car model is not sent: each device takes it from the catalog or from its own game.

Where it is kept and who sees it. On our server liverylab.uz (Cloudflare, a processor acting on our behalf), in Cloudflare R2 storage, under your account number. Only you can see and download it — by signing in with the same account. The cloud has no link and is not open to anyone else. Everything travels over HTTPS. The cloud holds up to 200 liveries, each up to 64 MB, and up to 2 GB in all per account.

How to delete. With "Delete" in the cloud window — the livery is erased from the server at once. Deleting your account erases the whole cloud.

Pro. The server keeps, under your account, whether you have a Pro subscription: where it was bought (App Store or Google Play), which product, until when it is valid and the original purchase number. To check a purchase, the server asks Apple or Google about it. We never see or receive payment details — card, address, amount.

How to delete your account

In the Android app and the Windows program: "Account" in the garage → "Delete Account" → confirm by signing in once more.

Erased at once:

  • the livery cloud and the subscription record — on our server
  • the account in Firebase — for good

What stays:

  • liveries on the device
  • links for a friend: they are not tied to the account and are deleted with "Delete link"
  • the subscription itself: it is cancelled where it was bought — in the App Store or Google Play
  • events in Amplitude — both those sent before signing in and those carrying the account number
  • crash reports: they are not tied to the account and are deleted automatically after 90 days

When you delete the account, an event about the deletion may go to Amplitude. The device on which you deleted the account gets a new number. On your other devices the number stays the same.

If the app is already gone, email us at nasriddinov.islom.19@gmail.com from the address you signed in with. We will reply to the address of your account and ask you to confirm the deletion, and we delete only after the answer comes from that very address — so nobody else can erase your account by asking. We will delete the account manually within 30 days. Right after you confirm, we will send Amplitude a request to delete the events tied to your account; Amplitude erases them within 30 days after the request. Write to us before you delete the account in the app: once it is deleted, we can no longer find the account number by your email.

Usage statistics and crash reports

The app counts what is done in it, and never what you painted. The statistics run in the iOS app from the App Store, in the Android app installed from Google Play, and in the installed Windows program. Developer builds, simulators, emulators and automated checks send nothing.

Which events. Which car was downloaded from the catalog: its catalog number, name, brand, class and game — this is public catalog data, not yours. For a car taken from the game's files, the name of its folder in the game. Importing a model of your own — only the file format. The same public car details also go with later events about that car — opening, exporting, the cloud: for a catalog car, its catalog name, brand, class and game; for a car from the game's files, the folder name; for a model of your own, the game and the ACC model name if the app guessed it, otherwise the mark "own". For a decal, where it came from is sent — a ready-made element (with its number in our library), text, recent, photo or file — but not the image or the text itself. Opening and closing a livery — how many seconds the work took, how many layers and decals were added, how many times you undid something and which tools you worked with. Exporting to the game or to an archive, sharing and a link for a friend — whether it worked, how many seconds it took and how many megabytes it weighs. The livery cloud — whether a livery was uploaded, downloaded or deleted and whether it worked; for an upload, also its size in megabytes. Signing in, signing out, deleting the account, the Pro window and a purchase — the step and the result. If an action failed, the kind of reason is sent — "no network", "no room" — not its text: the text can contain a path to your file.

What else is known about you. Sent with the events: the language in which you see the app, the number of cars and liveries in your garage, whether you are signed in and how, and whether you have your own paid Pro subscription and which — monthly or yearly. The free Pro that is currently open to everyone does not count as a subscription. In addition, Amplitude receives what it receives about any app: the device model, the system and app versions, the language and the mobile carrier; the Windows program sends the system name and version, its own version and the language.

Location — from the IP address. The IP address travels with the event, and Amplitude uses it to work out the country, region and city, approximately, as any website can. There is no precise location: the app does not ask for location permission and does not read GPS, latitude and longitude are not collected, and the advertising region is turned off.

Device number and account number. To tell one device from another, the installation is given a pseudonymous number. If you are signed in, the events carry one more pseudonymous number, calculated from the number of your Firebase account. It is the same on all your devices, so Amplitude can see that an iPhone, an Android phone and a computer belong to one person. No name or email can be seen behind this number in Amplitude, and the Firebase number itself does not go there. After you sign out, new events no longer carry this number, while those sent earlier stay as they were. When you delete your account, an event about the deletion may go to Amplitude. After the deletion, the device number is replaced with a new one — only on the device where you deleted the account — and new events from it are not tied to the earlier history.

What never goes into the statistics:

  • livery names and file names
  • the names you give to cars in your garage
  • the text you type into the catalog search
  • the text of inscriptions and decals
  • images
  • your name and email, and your Firebase account number

A crash report contains what such a report usually contains: where in the code the app crashed, the device model, the system version, free memory and disk space. A short marker about the last major action is attached — "editor opened", for example. There is no account number in a report.

Processors: Amplitude (statistics) and Google Firebase Crashlytics (crashes and remote settings). The processors act on our behalf; data is neither sold nor shared for advertising, and you are not tracked across other companies' apps: the app does not read the device advertising number and does not ask for tracking permission. Everything travels over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).

Windows version

The desktop program paints the same way as the phone app and keeps everything on your computer too: the library, liveries and decal pictures live in %APPDATA%\Livery Lab. Uninstalling the program leaves that folder alone — it is your work; you can delete it by hand.

Usage statistics in the installed program are the same as on phones: the same events, the location from the IP address and the pseudonymous account number after you sign in — all described in "Usage statistics and crash reports". The program sends them to Amplitude itself; Firebase Crashlytics is not used in the Windows version. The device number is random and is written to the settings file in the %APPDATA%\Livery Lab folder; uninstalling the program leaves that folder alone, so the number survives a reinstall until you delete the folder. A program launched by the developer from source code, and automated checks, send nothing.

Update check. On launch and once a day the program reads a file with the latest version number from liverylab.uz. The request carries the program and Windows versions in its header, like any program does — no installation number and no name. If you click “Update”, the program downloads the installer from the same place, checks its checksum and installs it over the old version.

Crash reports go to our own server liverylab.uz (Cloudflare, acting on our behalf), with no third-party services. A report contains the program, Windows and Java versions, the graphics card and driver name, the place in the code where the program crashed and the last lines of its log. File and folder paths — which contain your Windows user name and the names of liveries and pictures — are replaced with “<path>” before anything is sent. A report is tied neither to an account nor to a device, and the server does not store your IP address. Reports are kept for 90 days and then deleted automatically.

Otherwise the program goes online in the same places as the Android app: the model catalog, the link for a friend, sign-in and the livery cloud. Like that app, on every launch the program asks our server whether Pro is open and, if you are signed in, whether your account has Pro. If you are signed in, on launch the program also renews the sign-in: it takes the sign-in settings from auth.liverylab.uz and gets a fresh sign-in key, your name and email from Firebase. The program does not sell the Pro subscription — it is bought in the phone app with the same account.

Retention and deletion

  • Your files stay on the device until you delete them or delete the app
  • Links for a friend stay on the server until you delete them in the app or we take a link down after a report
  • Crash reports are kept by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days and then deleted automatically
  • Windows crash reports are kept on our server for 90 days and then deleted automatically
  • Usage statistics are kept in Amplitude for as long as the project runs, and serve only to understand what is used and what breaks

The account, if you signed in, is kept until you delete it — see "How to delete your account". The livery cloud and the subscription record are kept until you delete them yourself or together with the account.

There is no name and no email in the statistics. If you have never signed in, they are not tied to an account either, so we cannot find the records of a particular person in them. After you sign in, the events carry a pseudonymous account number. After you sign out, new events no longer carry it, while those sent earlier stay as they were. Deleting the account leaves new events from this device not tied to its earlier history, but the events already sent stay in Amplitude — without a name or email. How to ask us to erase them too is described in "How to delete your account". To stop anything leaving the phone, delete the app. On Android the installation number is deleted with it, and a new installation gets a new number unrelated to the old one. On iPhone the device number is the system's vendor identifier (IDFV): it stays the same as long as at least one other app from the same developer is installed on the phone, and a new one appears only when none is left. On a computer the number lives in the %APPDATA%\Livery Lab folder — delete it, and a new installation starts with a new number too. Any question about your data can be sent to the email below.

Sharing

When you build an archive and choose where to send it, the file is handed over by the system in the way you picked: AirDrop, Files, email, a messenger. That is your action and your choice of recipient — the app only hands the file to the system.

You send a link for a friend yourself in the same way: the app puts the address into the system share sheet or copies it, and you decide who gets it.

Purchases

Painting, building the archive and putting a livery into the game are free in every version. The only paid part is the Pro subscription (livery cloud, transfer between devices, the league grid), and until payments launch, Pro is free for everyone.

Once the subscription opens, it is bought in the App Store or Google Play: the store handles the payment, and the app never sees or stores payment details. The app passes the purchase confirmation to our server so that the subscription works on all your devices — what is kept is described in "Livery cloud and Pro". A subscription is cancelled in the store; deleting your account does not cancel it.

Children

Without signing in, the app receives no name and no email from anyone, children included; they appear only if someone signs in with Google. The usage statistics and crash reports described above contain neither a name nor an email: they know the approximate location from the IP address, and a pseudonymous account number appears in them only after signing in. A link-for-a-friend page shows only what was entered into the livery — if a child makes a link, a parent should know it can be deleted in the app.
